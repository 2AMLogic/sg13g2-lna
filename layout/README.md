## Scope

`lna_core/realization.json` is the single statement of scope. It must
partition `design/netlist/lna.spice` exactly into **three** lists
(`in_scope`, `modeled_in_device`, `out_of_scope`): `lvs_reference.py` and
`.github/scripts/check-signoff-scope.py` fail if any netlist instance is in
none, in more than one, or names a device the netlist no longer has.
`modeled_in_device` (issue #347) holds the three Q = 10 loss resistors
`Rle`, `Rlc`, `Rlb`: bench-model elements with no physical device, whose
physical counterpart is the drawn inductor's own series R and substrate loss
(`sim/models/SOURCE.md`). Drawing real resistors was rejected: it would change
the design's Q, which is a design change, not layout. They count as covered for
the partition and the scope guard, and emit no LVS reference card. Signoff
scope is `full` only when `out_of_scope` is empty.

**Drawn (35 of 38 instances; 3 more are `modeled_in_device`; none left
`out_of_scope`, so scope is `full`).** The first seven rows are the issue-#63
core and bias island; the matching network follows in "Matching network
(issue #347)" and the bias side in "Bias side (issue #346)" below. `full`
scope is a statement about what is drawn, not about LVS: the LVS reports
below are still `mismatch`.

**Core and bias island:**

| Instance | Drawn as (PDK PyCell) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `XQ1`, `XQ2` | `npn13G2`, `Nx` = the `nx` parameter (default 8, the netlist's value) | cascode core |
| `XQ3` | `npn13G2`, `Nx=1` | DR-0003 island: diode-tied density-match reference |
| `R3b` | `rppd` w=1u l=1.005u | DR-0003 island: `bref` to `b1` coupling. The PyCell's own model readout for this geometry is 329.74 Ω against the schematic's ideal 330 Ω |
| `Rbleed` | `rppd` w=1u l=386.650u | DC bleed of the output-match node, `xout` to `vss` (issue #258). Length is on the 0.005 µm manufacturing grid so the pad contacts pass klt 0.9.2's `cont.ongrid.1`; the 2 nm shortening moves r by ~0.5 Ω. The PyCell's own model readout for this geometry is 100.000 kΩ against the schematic's ideal 100 kΩ |
| `Cbref` | 16 × `cmim` 64.5 × 64.5 µm in parallel | DR-0003 island RF ground. 16 × 6.2507 pF = 100.01 pF at `cap_typ` against the ideal 100 pF. `cmim` allows at most 8 pF per instance |
| `XMis` | `pmosHV` w=512u l=1u, ng=32 (16 µm fingers) | DR-0003 island feed (the 12.8:1 bank into `bref`) |

"Bias island" is read the way DR-0003 uses the term: the `bref` node and
the chain on it (`Q3`, `R3b`, `Cbref`), plus `XMis`, the bank that feeds it.
The rest of the DR-0003 reference core, the `vb2` divider and the two
100 pF caps are drawn by issue #346 (next section). The nets that leave the
bias side are labelled ports of this cell: `b1`, `e1`, `outn`, `vb2`, `gsvo`,
`xout`, `vdd`, `vss`.

**The emitter count is a parameter.** `generate.py --nx N` (or `NX=N
layout/run_flow.sh`) redraws Q1/Q2 at `Nx=N`. Everything that depends on
the stripe span moves with it. When #58 lands, the change is one value,
not a redraw. Q3 stays at `Nx=1`. The island's density ratio is a design
quantity, and this script does not re-size it on its own.

matching network (netlist values from `design/netlist/lna.spice`; the PDK's
own PyCells, nothing hand-drawn at device level):

| Instance | Drawn as | Netlist | Drawn |
|---|---|---|---|

| `Cout` | 16 x `cmim` 64.5 um, top `outn`, bottom sheet `xout` | 100 pF | 100.01 pF |
| `Cpin` | `cmim` 20.375 um, top `xin`, bottom `vss` | 0.625995 pF | 0.62597 pF |
| `Cpout` | `cmim` 18.28 um, top `xout`, bottom `vss` | 0.504268 pF | 0.50416 pF |
| `Csout` | `cmim` 15.145 um, top `xout`, bottom `rfout` | 0.346489 pF | 0.34648 pF |
| `Le` | `inductor2` 4 turns w=8.22 s=3.74 d=30.13 um | 1 nH | 0.9999 nH |
| `Lc` | `inductor2` 5 turns w=6.1 s=3.29 d=109.15 um | 5 nH | 5.0001 nH |
| `Lb` | `inductor2` 4 turns w=8.22 s=3.74 d=151.95 um | 4.56374 nH | 4.5637 nH |

Capacitors use the same coefficients as `Cbref` (1.5 fF/um^2 area plus
0.04 fF/um perimeter); each cap's drawn-vs-schematic value is in its
`realization_note`. The bottom plate carries the substrate parasitic, so each
cap puts its bottom plate on the lower-impedance node; the schematic does not
model that parasitic, and no number here accounts for it.

**Inductor geometry and its evidence level.** `sim/models/SOURCE.md`: the
vendored model's EM correction is fitted at three extracted geometries only
(1, 4, 5 turns). `inductance_report.json` evaluates the model's
low-frequency series L (`em_ltot`) at each *drawn* geometry (read back from
the PyCell label in the GDS). A 1-turn cell tops out at 0.37 nH at the largest
extracted d, so `Le` (1 nH) and `Lb` (4.56 nH) are drawn with the 4-turn
extracted cell's w/s and a **solved d**: both are **not EM-verified**
(`em_verified: false`; `Lb` is 10 um beyond the extracted d, `Le` 112 um
below it). `Lc` uses the 5-turn extracted w/s at d 1 um from the extracted
point. The figure is a model evaluation, a low-frequency bound that says the
drawing matches the model's geometry, not a field solve of the GDS: klt
`mom` is a capacitance solve and cannot give inductance. Cross-check: the
unmodified `sg13g2_inductor_em.spice` run once in ngspice at 20 MHz for `Lc`'s
geometry gave 4.995 nH against the formula's 5.000 nH (0.1 %, the shunt
capacitance). Inductors are 250-410 um on a side; wiring from them to the core
and its parasitic R/L are not in the netlist numbers above.

**Connectivity was by label, not by routing, until #228 assembled the
blocks.** Each terminal carries a net-name label on
`TopMetal1.text`/`Metal1.text`/`Metal2.text`; #347 placed the blocks
(inductor row at y=50 north of the core; `Cin`/`Cout` banks east of `Cbref`;
small caps below them) without wiring them, so `klt extract` then reported
the shared names (`b1 e1 outn vdd vss xin xout`) as `split_net_labels` and
the scoped LVS reference could not pair those nets (the numbers in the
paragraph's original form are kept above as history: 54 mismatches after
#347, 85 after #346's bias side). The top-level assembly (#228, next
section) joined them; `split_net_labels` is now empty.

**Friction.** klt 0.9.2's sg13g2 extraction deck has no inductor class. The
spiral extracts as plain wire, so each inductor's two terminals come out as
one net (`merged_net_labels`: `e1|vss`, `outn|vdd`, `rfin|xin`), and
`lvs_reference.py` emits no card for `inductor2`. Filed generically as
`2AMLogic/klayout-tools#6726`.

**DRC (klt 0.9.2, `drc_report.json`): `clean`, 0 violations**, including the
new devices. DRC cleanliness is the deck's: the inductor's own `IND` marker
region is not checked against neighbouring metal by it.

**Spec untouched.** No L/C value, spec row or ratified number changed; the
PyCell geometries only realize the netlist's values.

## Top-level assembly (issue #228)

`draw_assembly()` in `generate.py` closes the block: it draws the four
`*.iopin` ports of `design/netlist/lna.spice` as PDK `bondpad` PyCells
(TopMetal2 octagons, 80 µm, `stack: nil`) and physically joins every
inter-block net. `realization.json` gained a `ports.list` key (the netlist's
own port list; `generate.py` fails if the two ever disagree).

**Routing architecture** (the `Router` class the bias channel uses, with the
assembly's layer pair): every terminal drops a **TopMetal2 spine** at unique
x down to its net's **TopMetal1 trunk** — one horizontal track per net in
the empty channel south of all blocks (y ∈ [-504, -440], 4 µm wide, 8 µm
pitch). TopMetal2 was empty before the assembly, so spines cross every block
and every other net's trunk with nothing under them; spines keep TopMetal2's
2 µm minimum space (the Router's collision check, raised to the TM2 rule).
Each terminal lands a `via_stack` from its own metal (Metal1, Metal2, the
bias Router's Metal3 trunks, the caps' Metal5 sheets/straps, or TopMetal1)
up to TopMetal2. `rfin`/`rfout` are two-terminal nets and take a direct TM2
run to their pads.

**Landing guard.** A via stack from, say, Metal1 to TopMetal2 draws pads on
every intermediate level; a landing is only legal where those levels are
empty. `draw_assembly` guard-checks every landing before drawing it: the
levels strictly between the landing's bottom and TopMetal2 must have no
geometry in the landing window (±2.5 µm, the measured ±1.93 µm pad plus
margin), or the generator fails loudly instead of shorting a net. The guard
is what pushed the cap-bank bottom-plate landings onto the banks' extended
west Metal1 fingers: the merged Metal5 sheets sit under the banks' own
TopMetal1 top plates, so no stack can cross there.

**Evidence (regenerated by `run_flow.sh`; GDS sha256 `c7f98160...`,
identical across two regenerations).**

- DRC (`drc_report.json`): `clean`, 0 violations — pads, TM2 spines, TM1
  trunks and all landing stacks included. The TopVia2/TopMetal2 rules now
  run over the assembly's own geometry: TopMetal2 goes 39 -> 138 shapes and
  TopVia2 346 -> 408 between the two GDS files (the pre-#228 shapes are the
  PyCells' own stacks, not drawn interconnect). The deck checked those rules
  before the assembly too — `coverage.rules_skipped` is empty and
  `coverage.checked` holds the same 63 rules in both reports — so what is new

- Extraction (`extract_report.json`): **`split_net_labels` is empty** —

  vdd xin xout` were each split across 2-3 nets). Net count 34 -> 18.
  `merged_net_labels` is unchanged (`e1|vss`, `outn|vdd`, `rfin|xin` — the
  three inductor spirals the deck has no class for, see "Friction" above),

- Scoped LVS (`lvs_report.json`): still `mismatch` — 85 -> 64 findings, the

## Provenance

**Every device is the PDK's own PyCell.** Each one comes from
`ihp-sg13g2/libs.tech/klayout/python/sg13g2_pycell_lib`, registered as
KLayout library `SG13_dev`. None is a hand-drawn footprint. `generate.py`
draws only the interconnect by hand. That means Metal1/Metal2/TopMetal1
wiring, a GatPoly bus that ties XMis's 32 gate fingers, a `ptap1`
substrate-tap rail under the `vss` rail, an `ntap1` well tap, the merged
Metal5 bottom plate of the Cbref bank, and net labels on the `<metal>.text`
layers (8/25, 10/25, 126/25). Those are the layers klt's sg13g2 extraction
deck reads for net names.

Running the PyCells headless needed three things the PDK install does not
provide:

1. **Two submodule shims.** The library imports `pypreprocessor` and
   `cni` (from `pycell4klayout-api`). IHP-Open-PDK carries both as git
   submodules, which a release-tarball fetch leaves empty. The PyPI
   `pypreprocessor` 0.7.7 sdist does not build on Python 3.14. On the
   development host, the `pycell4klayout-api` already in the PDK install was
   a different revision from the one v0.3.0 pins. `tools/fetch-pcell-deps.sh`
   therefore fetches both at the submodule commits the v0.3.0 tree records:
   `pypreprocessor@cf1ff9b` and `pycell4klayout-api@4c463c4`. It checks
   their sha256 and puts them in `layout/build/`. Nothing is installed
   host-wide.
2. **`KLAYOUT` in the environment.** The library keeps its `#ifdef KLAYOUT`
   code path only when that variable exists or a parent process is named
   "klayout". `pcell_env.py` sets it.
3. **No Tcl parameter callbacks.** `cni.dlo` runs `Calculate`-style
   parameter callbacks through tkinter and a `parameters.tcl` tree that the
   tarball does not ship. With tkinter importable, the first PyCell call
   raises and that instance draws nothing. `pcell_env.py` blocks tkinter
   while the library imports, and `generate.py` passes every parameter
   explicitly.

`klt gen --pdk-pcell` was tried first, as the klt-native route. It does not
vendor these shims, which is by design. With the shims put on `PYTHONPATH`
it crashes with an uncaught traceback instead
([klayout-tools#2944](https://github.com/2AMLogic/klayout-tools/issues/2944)).

**Reproducibility.** `generate.py` writes GDS with no timestamps and no
PCell context. Two runs give byte-identical output. The committed
`lna_core.gds` has sha256 `c7f98160…7615` (since #228's assembly; earlier
commits hashed `e2d75415…a14f` and then `4e1ef0ae…d833`), at nx=8, written with pip
`klayout` 0.30.12 (the version klt 0.9.2 runs, so one `klayout` serves both
legs). The GDS is byte-identical under 0.30.10 and 0.30.12 — both were run at
the 0.9.2 bump (#272) and agree — so `provenance.json`'s `klayout_python`
records which one wrote the committed bytes, not a constraint on
reproducing them. `lna_core.provenance.json` records that hash. It also records a sha256 over the PyCell library sources
(`sha256:6292d8c1…bb48`), the PDK's `.fetched-version` (0.3.0) and the two
shim commits. A different PyCell revision therefore shows up as a changed
hash rather than passing silently.

```bash
export PDK_ROOT=/path/containing   # the directory holding ihp-sg13g2/
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install klayout==0.30.12
PYTHON=.venv/bin/python layout/run_flow.sh
```

`run_flow.sh` runs klt as the **PyPI release** `klayout-tools==0.9.2`,
isolated through `uvx --isolated`. A host's installed `klt` can be a
post-tag build, and the development host's was (`0.7.0+g5c94de0ebbfe`,
`is_release: false`; at the 0.9.2 bump it was `0.9.2.dev1022+g2a9c52b27ce7`). A plain `uvx --from klayout-tools==0.9.2` silently
reuses an installed tool of the same version, so `--isolated` is
load-bearing here.

`run_flow.sh` also pins `klayout` itself (`KLAYOUT_PIN`, passed as `uvx --with
klayout==…`). klayout-tools requires `klayout` with no upper bound, so a bare
resolve takes PyPI's newest — 0.30.13 as of the 0.9.2 bump — while klt 0.9.2
declares `klayout_version_expected: 0.30.12`. Unpinned, a rerun would publish
evidence whose own `klt_version.json` records actual != expected — and klt's own
warning says deck `content_hash`, `rules_skipped` and `category_counts` move
with the engine even when the verdict does not. Pinning makes the run
reproducible; bump it when klt's expected version moves. The underlying gap (a
released klt pin has no supported way to reach a reproducible engine) is
[klayout-tools#5601](https://github.com/2AMLogic/klayout-tools/issues/5601).

`klt_version.json` records the producing build:
`0.9.2`, tag `v0.9.2`, commit `046ba74652fc`, `is_release: true`, klayout
0.30.12 (== expected).

### Native DRC runner (`run_native_drc.py`, #185 — evidence via #201)

IHP's own deck is the ground truth the curated report above cannot replace,
and this tree has no verdict from it. `layout/run_native_drc.py` is the
reproducible entry point that produces one, kept separate from
`run_flow.sh` because its prerequisites differ. It consumes the committed
`lna_core.gds` — it never regenerates or edits geometry — and publishes a
pair beside the curated evidence:

- `lna_core/native_drc_report.json` — the `klt drc --engine klayout --pdk
  ihp-sg13g2` envelope, verbatim (`clean`, `clean_partial`, `violations`,
  `coverage_unknown` or `not_checked` — the accepted completed-run set; on
  klayout-tools 0.9.2 the engine itself does not emit `clean_partial`,
  which is accepted for forward compatibility, issue #312);
- `lna_core/native_drc_inputs.json` — written by
  `layout/native_drc_identity.py` only after a completed run, binding the
  report's exact bytes to the GDS, to the PDK/deck identity the report
  itself records (copied verbatim from its provenance), and to the
  standalone executable version, the klt identity and the exact argv.

**Neither file exists in this tree.** The historical attempt failed because
the worker's standalone `klayout` was 0.28.16; producing the real verdict
needs an evidence host with a compatible executable, tracked as issue #201
along with the exact compatible version and the actual findings. The runner,
its preflight and its failure paths are the repository-side half (#185):
reviewed and PDK-free-tested now, so #201 is a run, not new tooling.

```bash
python3 -I layout/run_native_drc.py --min-klayout-version <x.y.z> \
    [--klayout-exe /path/to/klayout] [--pdk-root /path/to/containing/ihp-sg13g2]
```

Prerequisites, all explicit:

- **The standalone KLayout application** — the binary the deck shells out
  to, *not* the pip `klayout` module `generate.py` uses; installing the
  module neither provides nor updates it. Resolved from `--klayout-exe`,
  `$KLAYOUT_EXE`, then PATH.
- **A caller-supplied `--min-klayout-version`, with no default on
  purpose.** The deck's true minimum is only establishable on an evidence
  host, and inventing a number would let a wrong binary fail mid-deck (or
  worse, pass silently). The runner probes `-v` first and refuses with
  detected-vs-required; the same value is then passed to klt, whose own
  probe re-checks the binary it finds — the runner prepends the
  preflighted executable's directory to PATH so both probes hit the same
  binary.
- **klt 0.9.2**, the same pinned, `uvx --isolated` release as
  `run_flow.sh`; `check-klt-pin.sh` keeps the two pins from skewing.
- **The IHP PDK install** (`$PDK_ROOT`, or `--pdk-root`) so klt resolves
  the native deck for `--pdk ihp-sg13g2`.

Completed-run semantics (exit mapping per the klt 0.9.2 release docs):
`klt drc --engine klayout` exits 0 for clean and `clean_partial` (every
rule that ran passed, but the deck's requested rule coverage was partial),
3 for violations, and 4 for `coverage_unknown` — zero findings without a
caller-vouched rule-category assertion, which is how a genuinely clean
layout looks until a reviewed `--expect-rule-categories-file` is supplied
— and `not_checked` (the deck checked nothing). Accepted is not the same
as reachable: measured against the pinned producer itself (klayout-tools
0.9.2, issue #312), the klayout engine never populates `coverage.skipped`,
so a run reaches clean, `violations`, `coverage_unknown` and `not_checked`
today but not `clean_partial` — the gate accepts that token for forward
compatibility with klt's rollup contract, and it would arrive with exit 0
the moment klt populates `skipped`. The runner publishes all five verbatim with
the report's own status, so a `clean` native claim always traces to an
explicit recorded assertion in the binding's `argv`. **Honest-status
caveat**: a published `clean_partial`, `coverage_unknown` or `not_checked`
run is evidence, not a pass — none of them is full-scope DRC signoff, and
the runner says so in its completion output.
Any other exit is a failed run: nothing is published, the raw output is
kept in gitignored `native_drc_report.json.tmp`, and a previously
published pair is moved to `native_drc_stale/<utc-timestamp>/` first — a
failed rerun must never leave old evidence sitting in place as if it were
fresh. Publication is staged and refused unless the report pins the
committed GDS hash; each failure class has its own exit code (2 usage,
3 no executable, 4 unparseable `-v`, 5 below minimum, 6 deck failure,
7 publication self-check bug), listed in the runner's docstring. The
freshness gate (item 6 below) fails a one-sided pair — an interrupted
publication cannot pass silently.

**Scope.** Since #228 the cell covers all 38 netlist instances (35 drawn +
3 `modeled_in_device`), so a native verdict on it is a block-level result —
but there is still no verdict in this tree, and `native_drc_report.json`
stays uncited in `signoff/manifest.json` until #201 supplies the run and

## CI: layout-freshness

The `layout-freshness` job in `.github/workflows/signoff.yml` runs
`.github/scripts/check-layout-freshness` (self-test first:
`.github/scripts/test-check-layout-freshness.sh`). It needs only bash and
python3 stdlib: no klayout, klt or PDK. It fails, naming the relationship, if:

1. `sha256(lna_core.gds)` differs from `gds_sha256` in
   `lna_core.provenance.json`;
2. regenerating the LVS reference with `layout/lvs_reference.py` from the
   current `design/netlist/lna.spice` (in a temporary repo-shaped copy) is not
   byte-identical to the committed `lna_core.lvs_reference.spice` (this also
   catches a netlist edit that leaves a device outside the instance
   partition);
3. `provenance.input` in any of `drc_report.json`, `lvs_full_report.json`,
   `extract_report.json` or the scoped-LVS `lvs_report.json` is missing (or the
   report file itself is missing/unreadable), has a `role` other than `layout`, or has a `content_hash` that is
   not `sha256:` plus the committed GDS hash. This only pins the extraction and
scoped-LVS reports to the same GDS; it does not make them block signoff
evidence (the partial-scope guard above still applies);
5. the extraction output named by `extract_report.json` does not match the
   report. `netlist_path` is resolved relative to the report's directory (it
   must be a relative, repository-local path; absolute or escaping paths are
   rejected) and the file's raw bytes (not normalized SPICE) must hash to
   `netlist_sha256` (64 lowercase hex). A missing or malformed
   `netlist_path`/`netlist_sha256`, a missing output file, or a digest
   mismatch fails with a diagnostic naming the report and file. No klt or PDK
   is needed; this checks the committed pair, not that extraction would
   reproduce it;
4. `lna_core/lvs_inputs.json` (written by `layout/lvs_identity.py`, called from
   `run_flow.sh` only after a successful `klt lvs` invocation, exit 0 or the
   recorded mismatch exit 3) is missing/malformed, or the current bytes of an
   LVS report, its committed request, its reference netlist (path taken from
   the request: the scoped `lna_core.lvs_reference.spice`, or
   `design/netlist/lna.spice` for the full compare) or the GDS differ from what
   the recorded invocation used. The failure names which relationship changed.
   The sidecar is published atomically (tmp + rename) and only attests input
   identity: it never changes a report's verdict (both compares remain
   `mismatch`) and promotes no signoff row. If a run is interrupted between
   publishing a report and its sidecar entry, the report hash no longer matches
   and the gate fails rather than attesting the old report.
6. the native DRC pair `native_drc_report.json` + `native_drc_inputs.json`
   (written by `layout/run_native_drc.py` through
   `layout/native_drc_identity.py`, issue #185). Entirely absent is allowed —
   no native verdict exists in this tree until issue #201 supplies one — but
   a one-sided pair fails, and a present pair must: bind the report's exact
   bytes (`report_sha256`) and the committed GDS (`gds`/`gds_sha256`); agree
   with the report's own `status` (one of clean / clean_partial /
   violations / coverage_unknown / not_checked — the accepted set; on klt
   0.9.2 the klayout engine does not emit `clean_partial`, which is
   accepted for forward compatibility) and with the deck and PDK
   identity in the report's
   provenance (`deck_binding`/`pdk_binding`); record a non-empty klt identity
   and argv; and record a standalone executable version (`klayout_exe_version`)
   at or above the recorded `min_klayout_version`. The gate never runs the
   native deck and never requires the pair to exist; it only checks that
   what is committed is what the recorded invocation produced. A failed
   runner rerun never leaves the old pair in place (it is segregated to
   `native_drc_stale/`), and the pair names are only ever published
   atomically after the report has been validated against the GDS hash.

Regenerating the GDS and re-running the reports stays a deliberate local
step (`layout/run_flow.sh`); CI only checks that the committed files agree.
